The Paycheque Paradox

A bigger paycheque doesn't buy more time.

We laid off every one of the bank's 4,500 customers on paper, with EI paying 55% of their income. The people with the most money coming in ran out first. Income tells you how much flows through an account. Runway tells you how long someone lasts.

The king falls first. Big paycheque, tiny runway.

Out of money within 6 months of a layoff

EI at 55%, by income quarter

Why it happens

High earners spend almost everything that comes in, so EI at 55% leaves a large monthly hole and the buffer covers less than two months of spending.

Income against layoff runway

900 customers. Runway capped at 36 months for display. Select a dot to open it.

Customer6-month line

Runway predicts real defaults better than income

682 loans, 76 defaults. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect.

High earners do default less in real life (4.7% against 17.5% for the lowest quarter). The paradox is about what happens if the shock hits. Runway is the better measure either way.
