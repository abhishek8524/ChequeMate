How it works

Three moves. Checkmate.

Know the customer, take the shock, make the plan. Each one is a screen in the app.

Move 1

Know the customer

Six numbers from their own monthly history.

  • IMoney inmedian / month
  • SMoney outmedian / month
  • FFixed costsbills, insurance, loan
  • HHousehold billswhat rent shocks move
  • BSavings bufferavg balance, last 6 mo
  • PLoan paymentif still running
Move 2

Take a shock

Tap a twist, then drag it bigger or smaller.

Laid off, EI at 55%for 6 months. Do they make it?› The furnace diesrepair costs a month of spending› Layoff + bills up 15%in the same month›
Move 3

Make a plan

Two plans, priced in the customer's own dollars.

Goal planThe emergency fund for 3, 6, 9 or 12 months, and how far away it is.
Backup planIf the shock hits anyway: spend less, wait for income, or draw on the fund. Compared side by side.

The rule behind every answer

Runway = savings − one-off costsmoney out − money in

  • 0 months if one-off costs eat the savings
  • No shortfall if money in still covers money out
  • Otherwise capped at 120, one decimal

Biggest finding · The Paycheque Paradox

The king falls first.

On EI at 55%, 70% of the highest earners run out of savings before month six, against 4% of the lowest. Bigger savings, but losing 45% of a big income opens a gap they can't cover: $7,543 a month, against $309.

Validated

Our runway predicts real loan defaults.

The engine never saw loan outcomes. Still, borrowers with under 3 months of EI runway defaulted 5.5× as often as those with 6 or more.

One shock away

A quarter sit right on the edge.

25% of customers would last 3 to 6 months on EI, close enough that one repair bill decides it.

Surprise

Low unemployment isn't protection.

Prague has the lowest unemployment in the data, yet the second-highest share who fall short.

    Method and sources

    Built to be checked.

    Same definitions as the judges

    Medians of money in, money out and household bills from monthly_summary.csv; savings are the mean of the last six months' average daily balance. A random check of 300 accounts × 4 scenarios against the live server found 0 differences (scripts/verify_runway.py).

    Server is the source of truth

    Results are worked out on the server with exact decimals from the live database. The app shows a check on every Shock lab result, and the browser never touches the database.

    Sources

    • PKDD'99 Financial dataset, CTU Prague
    • EI regular benefits, Government of Canada
    • Consumer Price Index, Statistics Canada

    Scenarios are what-ifs, not EI eligibility estimates.
